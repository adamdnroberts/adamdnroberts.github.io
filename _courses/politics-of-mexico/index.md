## Course Description

This course introduces students to the politics of Mexico, from the consolidation of one-party rule under the PRI to democratization, alternation in power, and the contemporary challenges of violence, corruption, and democratic erosion. We will use Mexico as a case for broader debates in comparative politics: authoritarian durability, electoral competition, federalism, clientelism, organized crime, and the relationship between the state and society.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Describe the major historical and institutional developments in Mexican politics since the Revolution.
- Explain how the PRI sustained a hegemonic party regime and why it ultimately lost power.
- Analyze Mexico's electoral system, party system, and federal institutions.
- Evaluate competing explanations for criminal violence, corruption, and weak rule of law.
- Situate Mexico within comparative research on democratization and democratic backsliding.
- Write a short research paper that engages with scholarly debates on Mexican politics.

## Course Schedule

### Part I: Historical Foundations

- **Week 1:** Introduction — Why Study Mexico?
- **Week 2:** Revolution and State Formation
- **Week 3:** The PRI and Hegemonic Party Rule

### Part II: Democratization and Elections

- **Week 4:** Cracks in the Regime and the Road to Democratization
- **Week 5:** Elections, Electoral Institutions, and the Party System
- **Week 6:** Alternation in Power: The PAN, the PRD, and the Return of the PRI
- **Week 7:** Clientelism and Vote Buying

### Part III: State, Society, and Governance

- **Week 8:** Federalism, Subnational Politics, and Local Government
- **Week 9:** Organized Crime, Violence, and the Drug War
- **Week 10:** Corruption, Impunity, and the Rule of Law
- **Week 11:** Morena, Populism, and Democratic Erosion
- **Week 12:** Final Paper Presentations
